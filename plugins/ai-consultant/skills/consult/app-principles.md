# How we make apps for people

Principles every app we draw or build follows, from the first draft. The list will grow.

## 1. Show how the AI decided

When the AI reads, extracts, checks, scores or writes something, the person using the app has
to trust it before they'll rely on it. Trust comes from seeing the working, so whenever the AI
produces a value, a flag or a decision:

- **The source is one click away.** Show the document next to what was taken from it. Selecting
  a value shows where it came from: the page, sheet or table, with that spot highlighted.
- **Say how it got there.** One plain line per value: "Counted 14 joints in the circuit
  diagram legend, sheet 4", or "Flagged: the signature date is after the lodgement date".
- **Show how sure it is,** and draw the eye to the ones it's unsure about, so people check
  those first instead of everything.
- **People can correct it,** right there, and the correction is kept and visible ("changed by
  Sam").

In a draft, make this real for at least one value: a document preview beside the results,
and clicking a value highlights its source. That moment is often what convinces the client.

## 2. Say less

Every word on screen has to earn its place. One clear label beats a label and a caption; a
heading doesn't need a line underneath that repeats it. No subtitles under names, no hints that
restate the field, no "helpful" explanations of the obvious. If a screen needs a paragraph to be
understood, fix the screen. When in doubt, cut it, and see whether anyone misses it.

For example: the client switcher shows "Harbour & Partners", not "Harbour & Partners" with the
app's name underneath.
