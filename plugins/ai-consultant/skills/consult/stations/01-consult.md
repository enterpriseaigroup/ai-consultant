# 01 Consult

**Goal:** understand the job before anything is built: the real goal and who it's for, how the work happens today, and how we'll know it worked.
**Done when:** they agree with your goal sentence, the steps of today's process (at least three, or "no current process"), and 1–3 measures, each with today's number and a target.
**In BRIEF.md**, under `## 01 Consult`: `**Goal:**`, `**Who:**`, then `### How it works today` (numbered steps, each with who and which tool; mark the slow ones), then `### What good looks like` (the table `Measure | Today | Target`).

Take it in this order, one part at a time. Each part ends with a read-back they agree to. Move
on as soon as you have enough: don't ask whether there's more, and lead each next part with your
own read-back from what they've already said, for them to correct. Most people give you the goal,
today's process and the pain in their first two answers.

## 1. The goal

### From Mike's guides (`0_gofer_start`, `0a_problem_validation`)
- Establish what they want to achieve, who needs it and what currently makes it difficult. Use information already given; don't restart discovery when they've supplied it.
- Begin with the problem rather than a list of features.
- Identify whether they want a new idea, an improvement, help understanding a problem, or to continue an earlier discussion.
- Distinguish the stated problem from the proposed solution. Explore who experiences it, when it happens and its practical consequences.
- Use why questions selectively to find a useful cause. Don't force five repetitive questions, or present an inferred cause as proven.
- Reflect their goal back in one or two sentences, and let them correct it naturally.

### Added for this line
- "An interview app" is a solution. The goal is something like "get to a shortlist before good candidates take other offers". Two whys is usually enough; never more than three.
- If what they asked for won't fix the problem they describe, say so once, offer the alternative in a line, and let them choose.

- **No solution yet is fine.** If they only have a problem, the goal is the problem in one sentence and who it hurts ("Planning assessments take weeks, and applicants keep calling to ask why"). Don't push them towards an app.
- Your first reply introduces the four steps, then asks the opening question (see "Open with the four steps" in the skill).

**Opening question:** "What would you like to make easier for your team?" or, if they've named an app, "What's happening today that makes you want one?"

## 2. How it works today

### From Mike's guides (`1_gofer_research`, `0a_problem_validation`)
- Ask about current steps, handoffs, frustrations, accessibility needs, and exceptions when they change the design.
- Separate what you know, what you're assuming, and what's unanswered. Don't claim to have checked anything you haven't.
- Consider whether a simpler change to the process would help.
- Compare two or three approaches only when there's a real choice, and explain what each means for the people using it, not the technology.
- Finish with a short summary: the current journey, the main friction and the recommended direction.

### Added for this line
- **Ask for what shows how it works today**: a form, checklist, spreadsheet, email thread, procedure document, screenshots or a whiteboard photo. Draft the steps from it, then ask only about what it doesn't show. Note where each step came from ("from the intake checklist").
- If they don't know a step, ask who does, and add it under Open questions. Don't invent it.
- The slow step is usually where the app should start, and it isn't always the step they asked about.
- Draft the steps from what they've said and ask "Have I got that right?" rather than asking for each one.

**Question:** "Where do people most often get stuck, or have to chase someone?"
- Once the steps are agreed, ask who they'd sell this to: "Which clients would use this? Names or websites are enough." Write them under `## Clients` in BRIEF.md. It's optional; don't push.

## 3. What good looks like

### From Mike's guides (`0a_problem_validation`, `0_gofer_start`)
- Ask for real measures if they matter. Otherwise keep time savings, volumes and financial benefits as explicit assumptions, never invented facts.
- Explore the desired outcome and a simple measure of success.

### Added for this line
- One to three measures, not more. Each needs today's number and a target: without today's number you can't tell whether the app helped.
- If they don't know, offer two or three measures teams like theirs use (AskUserQuestion), with typical numbers. Mark them "(assumed)" and add them under Assumed.
- Measures are about the work, not the app: "days to a shortlist", not "number of logins".

**Question:** "If this worked, what would you notice? How long does that take today?"
- **If they're new to the area,** explain what a measure is for in one line ("so we can tell if the app helped"), then offer examples. Unknown baselines go under Open questions, with who could answer ("the planning team's monthly report").

### Before moving on: a short read-back

At the end of Consult, give a five-line summary in plain words: the problem, who it hurts, where it's slow today, how we'll measure it, and the open questions they should take back to their client or team. This is the consultant's deliverable, even if nothing gets built.
